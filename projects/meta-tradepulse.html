Stage Breakdown

What happens at each step

Data Collection

4 live APIs are pulled on a trading-day cadence: yFinance (OHLCV price data), FRED (macro indicators — VIX, 10Y yield, Fed Funds rate), PyTrends (Google search interest for "META stock"), NewsAPI (financial news sentiment). All sources are aligned to the trading-day index before feature engineering.

Feature Engineering (70+ features)

Technical indicators: RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, ATR. Macro signals: VIX level, yield curve spread, rolling correlation to S&P 500. Trend features: 7-day and 30-day Google search momentum. NLP sentiment: VADER + FinBERT scores on NewsAPI headlines, aggregated to daily signal.

5 Modeling Layers

Each layer is a diagnostic as much as a model: Layer 1 (OLS/AR) establishes the baseline. Layer 2 (regularized regression) checks for overfitting. Layer 3 (tree ensemble) captures nonlinear interactions. Layer 4 (PCA/PLS) handles multicollinearity in the 70-feature set. Layer 5 (GARCH/Kalman) models volatility clustering and time-varying parameters.

Walk-Forward Backtesting

Each model is validated with walk-forward (expanding window) backtesting: train on all data up to time T, predict at T+1, advance T by one period. This strict train/test boundary prevents lookahead bias — a critical requirement for any financial signal evaluation. Signal accuracy exceeded 59% across the backtesting window.

Key Decisions

Why it was built this way

Decision 1

5 progressive modeling layers instead of jumping to the most complex model. Running OLS first establishes a baseline R². If a GARCH model can't beat OLS on the same data, adding complexity is counterproductive. The progressive structure makes each layer's marginal contribution visible, which is more useful for understanding the signal than a black-box ensemble output.

Decision 2

Multi-source feature store over price data alone. Stock price alone severely underfits — the signal is almost entirely in the noise at daily resolution. Adding orthogonal signals (macro, search trends, sentiment) that are independent of each other and of price adds genuine predictive information rather than just adding correlated features.

Decision 3

Walk-forward backtesting over train/test split. A simple 80/20 train/test split on time-series data allows the model to implicitly learn future patterns if the test data is not strictly after the training data. Walk-forward evaluation is the standard in quantitative finance precisely because it simulates real-world deployment conditions.
